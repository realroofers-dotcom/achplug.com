What your customer sees

Three screens. Under two minutes. They never leave your site.

1. The box

Card price and bank price side by side, with your discount worked out. They press Pay from my bank.

2. The form

Name, email, routing number, account number. Their bank's name appears as they type. Repeat every month, if you allow it — for 12 payments, until a date, or until they stop. One sentence of authorization. Buy.

3. Thank you

Who they paid, at what address, how much, from which account, the reference — and that the receipt comes when it clears.

Their own page

Every email carries a link to a page that is theirs: every payment they've approved, Revoke on any repeating one, every receipt. No password.

Their account number is encrypted the moment it arrives. You see the last four digits. The full number appears only inside the file your bank reads.

One-time or recurring — a contract paid off, a service kept up, a monthly gift

Card processors charge the most for exactly this. It is built in.

For businesses

A deposit today. A roofing contract in 12 payments. A monthly service. A yearly membership. Switch repeats on in Settings; each payment appears on Collect by itself when it is due.

For nonprofits

A one-time gift, or a monthly donor. Donation mode takes the discount off, lets the donor pick the amount, and puts your EIN and the IRS acknowledgment line on every receipt.

For your customer

They approve once, on your site: every month, for 12 payments, or until a date. Before each one they get an email saying it is coming. Every email has Pause and Stop. Nothing is ever collected that they did not approve, and they can end it without calling anyone.

Charging extra for paying over time makes the sale a credit sale under federal law and needs disclosures; most sellers price the contract once and offer a discount for paying in full instead. That decision is yours — ACHplug handles either.

Why bank payments

No percentage.

A card processor takes about 3% and 30¢ of every sale. Your bank collecting an ACH payment costs cents, or nothing.

Nobody can freeze it.

A processor can hold your money for weeks on a complaint you never see. When your own bank collects, it is in your account and it is yours.

Recurring, with the customer in control.

A customer approves a repeat once — 12 payments on a contract, or a monthly service — and can pause or stop from the link in every email. Nothing is charged that they did not approve.

Nothing leaves your control.

Orders, the journal and the authorizations live in your dashboard. Your customers' account numbers are encrypted; you see the last four.
