One-time setup at your bank

Every business bank offers this. The names vary — "ACH Origination," "Business ACH," "ACH Manager," "Cash Management" — but the steps are the same.

  1. Ask your bank to turn on ACH origination for your business account. Call the business line or visit the branch and say: "I want to originate ACH debits — collect payments from customers who have authorized me." You'll sign an ACH origination agreement and they'll set a daily limit (ask for one that covers your busiest day).Some banks approve it the same day; some take a week. There is often a small monthly fee — $10 to $30 is typical.
  2. Get two things from them. Your Company ID — usually your EIN, sometimes an originator ID the bank assigns — and the routing number they use for ACH origination, which is usually your account's routing number.Ask exactly: "What Company ID and immediate-origin routing number go in my NACHA file header?"
  3. Enter both in ACHplug. Your desk → Settings → 6. Collecting. Save. The Download button on the Collect page appears.
  4. Send a test file. Most banks let you upload a file marked as a test, or will look one over for you. Do that once with a real approval of a dollar or two — your own account works — so the bank confirms the format before customers rely on it.
Keep the customer's authorization. ACHplug records it for you — the wording, the date, the IP address — and it's in the customer's email too. Banks can ask to see it if a customer disputes a debit; the Collect page keeps it for every payment.

Every day it's this

1Open Collect
2Download the file
3Upload it at your bank
4Press "mark all as sent"
5When it lands, press Received

Step 5 sends the customer their receipt and moves the payment to your Sales Journal. If a debit comes back — closed account, insufficient funds — press Returned instead, and ACHplug tells the customer.
